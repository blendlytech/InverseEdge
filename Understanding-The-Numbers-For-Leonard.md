## What we were really asking

There are two different questions, and it helps to keep them apart:

1. **"Are there patterns?"** — Can we find streaks, hot numbers, lucky shapes in the past results?
2. **"Are there patterns we can use to win money going forward?"** — That's the one that actually matters to your wallet.

The answer to #1 is "sort of, but not the useful kind." The answer to #2 is "no — and there's a clear reason why." Let me walk you through it gently.

## A coin, a pair of dice, and your lottery

Picture flipping a coin 10 times. You might get **H-H-H-H** — four heads in a row. Your eye says, *"It's on a streak! Heads is hot!"* But you and I both know the coin has no memory. The next flip is still 50/50. The streak was real, but it didn't *mean* anything. It couldn't help you guess flip number eleven.

The Pick-3 lottery is the same idea, just with a 10-sided "coin" (the digits 0 through 9) drawn three times. Each draw is its own fresh event. The machine does not remember yesterday. It does not know a number is "due."

When we checked your 364 real draws, that's exactly what we saw:

- Every digit, 0 through 9, showed up just about equally often.
- Where a digit landed (first, middle, or last spot) was even.
- Yesterday's numbers told us **nothing** about today's.
- After a number sat "cold" for a while, it was **no more likely** to come back than any other.

In plain terms: your lottery behaves like a perfectly fair pair of dice. And nobody can out-guess a fair pair of dice.

## Your "high numbers" idea — we tested it directly

You've felt that numbers made of **high digits (5, 6, 7, 8, 9)** come up more often. This was important to you, so we tested it carefully against your real draws.

Here's what we found:

- Numbers made of only high digits came up **26 times** out of 364.
- Numbers made of only low digits came up **18 times**.

So at first glance — *you're right!* High beat low, 26 to 18. I can see exactly why you believe it.

But here's the catch, and it's the same catch as the coin flips. **That gap is the size of ordinary luck.** If you flip two fair coins all day, one will land heads a bit more than the other — that doesn't make it a "better" coin. When we measured whether 26-vs-18 was a *real* effect or just normal wobble, it came out as **normal wobble.** Run the lottery again next year and the low numbers might edge ahead instead. There's no real lean toward high numbers — it just looked that way in this batch, the way four-heads-in-a-row looks like a streak.

## The part that costs money (this is the big one)

Let's say you didn't believe any of that, and you decided to play the high numbers every single day anyway. We added up what would have actually happened over those 364 draws:

- You'd play the 10 "all-high" combinations each draw — about **$10 a day**.
- Over 364 draws, that's roughly **$3,640 spent.**
- You'd have won on those 26 days. At Colorado's box payout of **$80** on a $1 ticket, that's about **$2,080 back.**
- **Net result: you'd be down about $1,560.**

And this isn't bad luck — it's built into the game. The lottery only pays you a fraction of true odds. It's like a store that sells you a $1 item for $2: no matter *which* item you pick off the shelf, you're overpaying. Choosing the "high number" shelf doesn't change that — every number is overpriced the same way.

## The prize you really want — the Exact ($500 straight)

Leonard, I know the **exact** win is the one you're truly after, and I understand why — in Colorado a $1 straight pays **$500**. That's the big one. So let's be just as honest and careful about it as everything else.

To win the straight, you have to name all three digits **in the exact order** they come out. There are 1,000 possible exact numbers (000 through 999), so any single ticket has a **1-in-1,000** chance. A $500 prize on 1-in-1,000 odds means that, over the long run, for every $1,000 you spend you'd get about $500 back — the lottery quietly keeps the other half. And that's true **no matter which number you pick**, because all 1,000 are equally likely.

Here's the hard part for any pattern system. To actually come out *ahead* on the straight, a number would have to hit about **twice as often as it should** — roughly 1-in-500 instead of 1-in-1,000 — and keep doing it, draw after draw. We went looking for exactly that in your numbers:

- Are any exact numbers running hot? *(No — the most frequent showed up 4 times in 364 draws, which is just what luck sprinkles around.)*
- Do certain digits favor the first, middle, or last spot? *(No — every spot is even.)*
- Does the midday draw behave differently from the evening draw? *(No — they look the same.)*

There's one more honest catch, and it's a big one: to even *prove* a straight number was truly beating the odds, you'd need to watch **many thousands** of draws — not a few hundred. With 364 draws, the only edge we could possibly spot would have to be huge and obvious. There's nothing like that here. The straight is the hardest prize to win and the hardest place to find any pattern — for the same reason: every one of those 1,000 numbers is a fair, equal shot, every single draw.

## "But I've SEEN patterns!" — and you have

Here's the honest, gentle truth about that. Our brains are **pattern-finding machines.** It's how our ancestors survived — spotting the shape of a lion in the tall grass. That same wiring makes us see faces in clouds, hear our name in a noisy room, and feel certain a lottery number is "due."

When you win, you remember the pattern that "called it." When the same hunch loses, it quietly fades from memory. That's not a flaw in you — it's how every human mind works. It's also exactly what casinos and lotteries count on.

So the patterns you've seen are **real to your eye** — but they're the lion in the clouds, not a lion in the grass.
